How to run it

The week

Days 1 and 2 are the main-lift days: the two competition lifts, warm-up, and nothing else. They should fit in about an hour. Days 3 and 4 are the accessory days, done back to back. Each one leads with a variation of a main lift (paused squat on the lower day; a bench variation and overhead volume on the upper day), then the accessory list. Accessory days don’t count toward the tonnage progression. The accessory list stays the same until the peak.

  • Keep at least 48 hours between Day 1 and Day 2.
  • Do the lower accessory day first. It sits 2 days after deadlift and 3 days before squat, which keeps the paused squat away from both heavy days.
  • The upper day can fall the day before Day 1. Overhead press recovers fast enough.
  • If a week gets squeezed, drop the upper accessory day first, then the accessory list on the lower day. Keep the paused squat if you can.
Warm-up

Main-lift days (about 12 minutes): five minutes easy on the bike, then the McGill big three — curl-up, side plank, bird dog — in short holds of about 10 seconds, descending reps (for example 5-3-1 per side). Add hip airplanes or 90/90 switches, 5 per side.

Ramp the first main lift: bar ×10, 40% ×5, 60% ×3, 75% ×2, 85% ×1 before anything heavier. The second main lift needs only two or three ramp sets.

Accessory days: bike and the McGill big three, then two or three ramp sets for the lead variation.

Protecting your back
  • Squat and deadlift are never heavy on the same day. All loaded hinging happens on Day 2, so your erectors get 4–5 days between hard exposures.
  • The lower accessory day has no loaded hinge. Back extensions there are light and stop well short of failure.
  • Belt on every set at or above 80%.
  • Deadlift is capped at RPE 8.5 until test day. Stop any set when the brace leaks; that rep doesn’t count anyway.
  • No heavy hinging in the first hour after waking.
  • If your back feels off during warm-up, swap that day’s deadlift for block pulls just below the knee or trap bar at the same RPE, and drop the back extensions that week. If it spasms, run that week as a deload rather than training through it.
  • The McGill work stays in the warm-up even when you’re short on time. It’s the cheapest insurance in the program.
Percentages, RPE and updating maxes

Percentages are of your estimated 1RM. Top singles are prescribed by RPE; the load shown next to them is what that RPE should look like if your max is accurate.

If a top single lands a full RPE harder than prescribed, take 5% off the back-off sets that day. If it lands a full RPE easier, add 2.5%.

Update your maxes with the calculator after week 1 (calibration sets) and at the last hard week of each block (weeks 7, 15 and 23). Week 26’s heaviest singles set your test-day attempts.

Cycling alongside

Two Zone 2 rides and one interval session a week: Zone 2 on Tuesday and Sunday, intervals on Thursday. Thursday is the only day that isn’t within 24 hours of Day 1 or Day 2, so keep intervals there if the week shifts.

During the peak (from April 5), shorten the interval session. From April 19: Zone 2 only, 45 minutes or less, and nothing on April 26.

Test day

Order: squat, bench, overhead press, deadlift. Three attempts each, 5–8 minutes between attempts, and about 15 minutes between lifts. Use the same warm-up and ramp as the openers in week 27.

Run it like a meet: squat to honest depth, pause bench on a spotter’s “press” command, record every attempt. Take the second attempt off how the opener moved: fast opener, go to the planned second or a bit above; grindy opener, repeat-plus-5.
